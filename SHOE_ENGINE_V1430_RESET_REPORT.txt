SHOES ENGINE CLEAN RESET REPORT

ARCHITECTURE
The previous layered Shoes planner was removed and replaced with one authoritative planning path.

The new engine processes:
1. Current owned physical pairs and their actual mileage.
2. All future training + rehabilitation shoe-requiring sessions.
3. Per-session suitability scores for every genuinely available physical pair.
4. A weekly rotation optimiser.
5. Lifecycle capacity / retirement.
6. Purchase need.
7. Race Day footwear.
8. Final assignment ledger and graph.

CORE RULES
- Every session is ranked by shoe suitability.
- Initial assignment is the highest-scoring available pair.
- Every principal available pair targets >=25% of weekly shoe mileage.
- The 25% balancing optimiser minimizes race-value-weighted suitability loss.
- Threshold/interval/race-specific/long-run and return-to-run rehab sessions carry greater importance.
- Easy/recovery/lower-value sessions are therefore moved first when the 25% rule requires a compromise.
- Manual runner choices remain locked.
- Future physical pairs are unavailable before first use.
- A shoe can never be assigned beyond its planning-retirement mileage.
- Target portfolio is 2–3 useful physical pairs.
- A purchase is created only when the rotation would fall below two useful pairs while meaningful programme mileage remains, or when no available pair can safely cover a session/lifecycle need.
- New ordinary pairs are not created merely because another model scores slightly higher.
- Race Day pair must have <250 km before the race, sufficient familiarisation and enough lifecycle remaining for the race.
- An owned or already-required training pair is evaluated for Race Day before a dedicated race pair is purchased.
- Graph mileage is derived only from the final physical-pair assignment ledger.
